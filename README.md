## Why This Book Exists

Every day there's a new AI model, a new tool, a new headline about jobs being replaced. The noise is deafening. The anxiety is real.

But almost everything written about "AI and the future" falls into one of three buckets:

1. **Technical manuals** — how to code with AI (useless if you're not a developer)
2. **Productivity hacks** — how to write emails faster (helpful but shallow)
3. **Doomscrolling** — "AI will take your job!" (true for some, but offers no path forward)

**This book is different.** It's not about how to use AI. It's about **how to be a human** when AI can do almost anything.

It's written for:
- The office worker wondering if they'll be laid off next quarter
- The small business owner trying to stay competitive without a tech team
- The freelancer watching AI flood their market with cheap alternatives
- The student picking a major and a career path
- The mid-career professional whose industry just got disrupted
- The person approaching retirement who just wants to keep up

**No coding required. No hype. No panic.**

## The Core Thesis

> AI时代的终极竞争力，不在于"比AI做得更好"，而在于"做AI不能做或不该做的事"。

In English: **Your irreplaceability doesn't come from doing things better than AI — it comes from doing what AI cannot or should not do.**

AI doesn't have a body. It hasn't suffered. It doesn't know what it's like to lose someone, to hold a child, to walk in the rain, to make a choice that keeps you up at night. These aren't bugs — they're the boundaries of what AI is. And they're exactly where you live.

This book helps you find that line, then build your life on the right side of it.

## How to Read This: Text vs. Appendices

The chapters are about **how to think**. The appendices are about **what to use**. They age at
completely different rates, and that is deliberate.

| | Chapters | Appendices |
|---|---|---|
| Answers | "How should I think about this?" | "Which tool, at what price, right now?" |
| Ages | Slowly — arguments, not products | Fast — monthly, sometimes weekly |
| Free? | Parts I–II are free | Paid layer |

So the book does **not** claim its tool matrix is authoritative. It can't be, and Ch. 21 exists to make
that explicit: it teaches you to judge a tool yourself, so that when Appendix A goes stale — and it
will — you are not stuck waiting for someone else to update it. **The appendices are a convenience;
the judgement is the product.**

## Why a "Living Book"?

AI changes weekly. A printed book is obsolete before it ships.

This is a **living document** — version-controlled like software, updated like a newsroom. Every chapter carries a `Last Updated` timestamp and model version. When a tool dies, we strike it. When a new model drops, we test it. When the law changes, we note it.

You're not buying a static PDF. You're subscribing to a **survival intelligence system**.

## How It's Made (Yes, AI Helps)

This book is **co-authored by humans and AI**, in public:

```
Intel Sources (RSS / X / ArXiv / Product Hunt)
        ↓
    AI Summarizes (Perplexity / NotebookLM)
        ↓
    DeepSeek drafts chapters
        ↓
    Reasonix agent manages files & builds
        ↓
    Human editor (me) reviews, fact-checks, judges
        ↓
    GitHub Actions opens PR → I merge → Build & deliver
```

Every chapter footer discloses:

```markdown
---
📅 Last updated: 2026-09-30
🤖 Assisted by: DeepSeek + Reasonix
✍️  Edited by: Human (that's me)
⚠️  Verify critical facts yourself — AI moves fast, I do my best.
---
```

**Transparency is a feature, not a bug.** If I'm writing a book about living well with AI, I should probably be using AI to do it.

## Repo Layout

```
chapters/en/          The book text. Source of truth — translations derive from here.
  README.md           Chapter index + per-chapter status
appendix/             Monthly-refreshed tool matrices and prompt library
templates/            Chapter template (front matter + required footer)
scripts/              Local tooling: lint, publish, SSH setup, gh bootstrap
WORKFLOW.md           How a chapter gets written and shipped
CHANGELOG.md          What changed, by date
```

**Start here if you want to write:** [WORKFLOW.md](WORKFLOW.md).

Quick version:

```bash
cp templates/chapter-template.md chapters/en/ch02-your-slug.md
# ...draft it...
./scripts/check-chapter.sh  chapters/en/ch02-your-slug.md   # lint
./scripts/publish-chapter.sh chapters/en/ch02-your-slug.md  # branch → commit → push → PR
./scripts/pr-merge.sh draft/ch02-your-slug                  # merge, once it reads right
```

One chapter per branch per PR, self-merged. Content is English-first.
